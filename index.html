01 — Understanding

What you're solving for

Saturday enquiries land in respond.io when nobody is at a desk. By Monday the lead has cooled or gone to a competitor. You want a first response within minutes, in your voice, without handing control of the conversation to a bot for good.

REQUIREMENT
Saturdays only, SGT

The bot is silent six days a week. respond.io's Date & Time step decides, so the rule lives where your team can see it.

REQUIREMENT
Owner-editable, no Make

Mode, assignee, reply cap and the full bot instructions sit in a Google Sheet. Knowledge files sit in a SharePoint folder.

REQUIREMENT
One reply per burst

Customers send four short messages in a row. They should get one considered answer, not four.

REQUIREMENT
Clean handover

After replying, the bot tags the conversation, assigns it to a named team member and leaves an internal note. Humans take it from there.

04 — Reliability

Built so no lead is dropped silently

01

Fails to a human, never to silence

If Claude, SharePoint or the respond.io API errors, the conversation is still tagged and assigned, with a comment saying the bot couldn't answer.

02

Reply cap as a safety valve

A daily cap in the sheet stops runaway cost or a loop. Once hit, the bot goes handover-only for the rest of the day.

03

No double replies

A per-conversation lock in Make's data store means two messages seconds apart can't trigger two Claude calls.

04

Prompt caching from day one

Instructions and knowledge files are the cached prefix. Only the conversation varies, so repeat calls pay a fraction of the input price.

05

Singapore time, end to end

Saturday is checked in Asia/Singapore in respond.io and re-checked in Make, so a UTC rollover can never fire the bot on a Friday night or Sunday morning.

06

Yours, in your accounts

Make scenarios, Anthropic key and respond.io workflow all live under your logins. If we part ways, nothing stops working.

07 — The same pattern, elsewhere

Two worked examples of this architecture

Illustrative scenarios, not client case studies. They show how the same building blocks, a channel trigger, Make in the middle, Claude with cached context, and an owner-editable sheet, bend to different businesses.

WORKED EXAMPLE · DENTAL CLINIC

After-hours WhatsApp triage with next-morning handover

Patients message outside opening hours. respond.io checks the time window, Make pulls the thread and the clinic's FAQ document from a shared drive, Claude answers routine questions (hours, parking, what to bring) and flags anything that sounds urgent. Every conversation is tagged by urgency and assigned to the front desk, who open a prioritised queue at 8am. The practice manager controls the hours window, escalation keywords and the assignee from a Google Sheet.

Time-window gate Urgency tagging Sheet-driven settings
WORKED EXAMPLE · PROPERTY AGENCY

Weekend viewing enquiries, drafted for agents to approve

Listing enquiries spike on Saturdays and Sundays while agents are out on viewings. Make batches each burst of messages, fetches the listing sheet so Claude knows price, size and availability, and drafts a reply as an internal note rather than sending it. The agent on duty reads the draft on their phone, taps send or edits it. A reply cap and a per-agent rota live in the same sheet, so the office manager can reassign weekends without touching the automation.
